Wardline

Follow-up care that keeps up with the people providing it.

Problem

Chronic-care follow-up is stretched thin.

When teams are short-staffed, urgent requests can crowd out routine follow-up. A missed call or video visit can make it harder to stay in touch after discharge.

  1. Outreach. Reach each patient.
  2. Status check. Find out how the patient is doing and what has changed.
  3. Notes. Write down what happened and transfer the notes into the hospital’s system.

These observations come from the founder’s pitch sessions and idea analysis.

Product

How Wardline could work

Fit the hospital’s workflow

Wardline Health envisions an assistant that would learn a hospital’s own care protocols, escalation steps, and documentation patterns over time.

Support follow-up after discharge

  1. A daily briefing would give nurses and care coordinators a short view of upcoming visits and relevant context from previous interactions.
  2. After a visit, a nurse could log what happened by voice or text. The goal is to reduce the need to write notes on paper and enter them again later.
  3. Patients could have another way to stay in touch between visits. The concept includes exploring support for follow-up in multiple languages.

Wardline Health is still defining the first buyer, the budget owner, and how to measure return on investment.
